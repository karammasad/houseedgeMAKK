## Post-hoc diagnosis (after unblinding; the public kit was regenerated byte-identically with hidden structure / flip labels; `diagnose.py`)
- The loss is localized: **no-gate machines** (85 of 1,536): T 277.9 vs F 108.6, F - T -169.4 [-301.6, -58.7]; steady no-gate (always-win, true rate .99): T 529.5 vs F 122.4. Thanos has no no-gate structure in its support (the screen gives it zero mass even at threshold 0, because for a constant-gate-bit machine every gate ties with 'none' and ties go to the gated structures), so an always-win machine is forced through on/off priors.
- Excluding no-gate machines Thanos is much better: gated only 32.2 vs 48.6, F - T +16.4 [+11.3, +21.7]; stable +19.8 [+10.3, +31.9], drift +15.0 [+8.6, +22.7], shared_change +19.8 [+11.5, +29.5], short_history +11.1 [-1.7, +23.5]; pairs and single-lag gates equally (+16.4, +16.5).
- Minor, small-n: drift machines flipping before 45% of the log (11 machines, tau/L < .45): T 229 vs F 24 (window assumption: hyperparameters are fitted on the first 45%); the other drift bins favour Thanos or are mixed.
## Verdict
Strictly by the pre-registered rule: NO-GO for Thanos E2 UNCHANGED as the Round 2 primary. The failure is one identifiable, fixable mode, not general weakness: outside no-gate machines Thanos wins by about a third. Smallest justified adaptation (PROPOSED, NOT BUILT): add the no-gate candidate to the support (one extra structure, nested: EM can set its mass to ~0 and recover the original), or route machines whose gate evidence is degenerate (win rate > .97 or < .03) to the fallback. Must be validated on a fresh kit or real-train CV before use; this kit's test outcomes are now spent for that purpose. Caveats: one synthetic kit from my own generator (no-gate share 5% and rates from Round 1 fits; Round 2 may differ), both models fit concurrently.
