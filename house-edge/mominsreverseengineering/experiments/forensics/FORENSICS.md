# Residual forensics of v9 + shared-base (train-only, cached fit, no refit, no audit/validation labels)
Data: 22,560 pseudo-queries (targets at ticks 30%-100% of 576 long train machines, history = ticks before the target) + 768 real train outcomes (labels.csv, never read by fit). Model under test: shared-base gate + size model (stackers excluded).
Sanity check of the earlier "40.3%": numerator 50,076 (sum of squared distance to the TRUE simulated distribution, x1e-4, 10 machines), denominator 124,188 (all 2,270 gated machines). It is TOTAL error, not excess. Excess over the tau-known predictor: 49,884 / 77,973 = 64%. It was a simulator result with flips allowed up to the cutoff, which the real generator does not do (below).
## Step 1: no systematic residual
Overall win residual +0.0012 +- 0.0026. Five-category reliability: each category within 0.003 of observed. Win-prob bins: 7 of 8 within noise ([.10,.30) +0.018, z 2.5; [.97,1) -0.017, z -2.7). 49 slices (type, condition, signal, structure form, pair, u/L, time since flip, top-structure mass, last payout, payout at size lag, ticks since last >=5x): none replicates with |z|>=3 except gate off-rate [.2,1) (+0.022, z 3.1). That one is an ARTIFACT: strength was measured on the full history including the target. Recomputed from the prefix only: off-rate [.2,1) +0.0010 (z 0.2); every prefix on/off-rate bin |z|<1. Size given win by last payout: predicted vs observed within 0.02 everywhere. Real train outcomes: residual by condition +0.045 (z 2.1) for stable only, not seen in 7,488 stable pseudo rows (+0.002): chance among 4 looks.
## Step 2: generator relationships
1. Flip regime is set by the episode condition (production posterior, 576 long machines): stable 2/192 flipped (no flips); drift 192/192, tau/L 0.53-0.84; shared_change 192/192, tau/L 0.58-0.77, and ALL machines of an episode flip at the same tick (within-episode std 1.0 ticks vs 16.8 under permutation; log Bayes factor for a shared tau median +31, 24/24 episodes); drift: partial clustering (std 13.1 vs 20.5). short_history: 97% of short machines have P(flip)<.1 and the learned p_change_short sits at the grid floor .05, so the model already treats them as nearly flip-free.
2. Real flips never occur in the last ~16% of the log (max tau/L .84), so the simulator's late flips (the 40% error) do not exist in the real generator.
3. Gamma, payout size, gate strength: no residual dependence found (above).
Residual error does concentrate at the flip (rows within 10 ticks of tau: 13% of rows, 25% of Brier, 2.3x), but real queries sit at the end of the log, at least 38 ticks after the flip.
## Step 3: breakthrough candidate
Strongest pattern: the condition-driven flip regime (shared tau in shared_change). Evidence is strong (24/24 episodes) but it is already captured: each long machine's own tau posterior is within ~1 tick, short_history prior is already ~.05, and a regime prior could only help where the flip posterior is diffuse (short logs, which are flip-free). Upside ~0, cost low. No credible unexplained pattern remains in this diagnostic. VERDICT: no breakthrough; remaining error is per-machine rate noise (Adi: jackpot on-cell residual sd .130 vs true-rate sd .164), which these residuals do not expose any structure in.
